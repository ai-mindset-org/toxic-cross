# Krest – product, distribution and reflection research – 2026-10-16

Build a deliberately small, inspectable “day ledger”: manual commitments/resources plus optional calendar context, an explainable planning indicator, and opt-in reflection. Choose a native macOS menu-bar app with one local engine and an optional single-user self-hosted dashboard; do not launch a cloud mood platform. The strongest precedents establish distribution and transparency patterns, not psychological efficacy. [ActivityWatch architecture](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/architecture.html) [Maccy installation](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/README.md)

Evidence cutoff: 2026-10-16. The 25 primary sources below span more than eight domains and include Dutch, Hungarian and French/Canadian research. Dates are publication/issue dates where established; undated documentation is a retrieved snapshot, not proof of when functionality shipped. Academic studies support cautious measurement and context-sensitive reflection, not validation of this product. Project/platform documentation establishes stated behavior and requirements, not independent security assurance. Recommendations below are explicit product judgments; all suggested defaults and the balance formula require usability testing. No purchase, account creation, contact or ongoing monitoring is needed for this proposal.

## Decisions applied

1. **Position v2 as an experimental, single-user day-planning and reflection tool—not a mood detector, wellbeing score, burnout predictor or therapy. Ship a useful manual-only mode before asking for permissions.**

Fact: ActivityWatch separates collectors, storage and a web UI; Maccy offers source, GitHub downloads and Homebrew. These demonstrate inspectable distribution patterns, not demand or efficacy. [ActivityWatch architecture](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/architecture.html); [Maccy README](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/README.md)

Recommendation: headline “See your commitments and resources; optionally record how an event felt.” Label every build “Experimental beta: rules and data formats may change; not a health assessment.”

Limits: No retrieved study evaluates this product. Open source, a polished menu bar and published research do not establish clinical accuracy or improve wellbeing.

2. **Keep one local data store and rule engine, with a native menu-bar client and an optional self-hosted dashboard. Default to no account, cloud sync, analytics, AI provider or remote access.**

Fact: ActivityWatch documents local storage and optional external requests, while its security page warns that a reachable unauthenticated API can expose all data. [Privacy](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/privacy.html); [Security](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/security.html)

Recommendation: local SQLite, versioned records and one authoritative evaluator; use native IPC where practical. If HTTP is needed, bind loopback, require a secret, validate Origin/Host, protect against CSRF and never put secrets in URLs. Serve dashboard assets locally. Self-hosting must be an explicit export/sync destination with authenticated TLS—not a default LAN listener.

Limits: Local-first is not local-only or automatically encrypted. Malware, another privileged process, backups, browser origins and remote-host administrators remain threats; do not copy ActivityWatch’s default unauthenticated boundary.

3. **Publish a readable threat model and a literal data/network inventory alongside the privacy notice. Make deletion, export and permissions visible in the product.**

Fact: Apple recommends data minimization and understandable permission requests; ActivityWatch discloses release checks and optional AI rather than equating local storage with zero network traffic. [Apple privacy guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy); [ActivityWatch policy](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/privacy.html)

Recommendation: inventory calendar title/start/end/calendar ID, manual entries, ratings and notes; omit attendees, locations and descriptions unless separately enabled. Show storage paths, retention, backup behavior and every outbound destination. Offer JSON/CSV export, per-event deletion, clear-all, import preview and a “what leaves this device?” panel. Calendar deletion inside this app must not delete the source calendar event.

Limits: Disclose update checks, release downloads and OS services separately from product telemetry. Deletion cannot retract exported or backed-up copies; do not promise secure erasure from SSDs or protection from a compromised Mac.

4. **Make “balance” a transparent planning heuristic with separate load, resources and uncertainty—not a psychological measurement.**

Fact: EMA studies measure subjective responses under specific protocols; even validated adolescent well-being items showed ceiling effects and were not validated in actual EMA deployment. [EMOWI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8739680/)

Recommendation: user-entered obligations/resources carry category, time horizon, optional duration and an editable 0–3 planning weight. Display weighted load L, resource R and signed difference R−L; disclose that unlike units are user-defined. Keep past observations, present commitments and future plans in separate lanes. Show unknown/unweighted items and coverage, not a falsely precise percentage. Every indicator opens its contributing items, weights and rule-version explanation. Emotional ratings never silently alter the rule score.

Limits: The proposed arithmetic, weights and labels are product choices, not validated scales. No universal “healthy” threshold, population comparison or causal attribution; disable the indicator if inputs are too incomplete or the user prefers a plain list.

5. **Implement explicit event phases and stable occurrence-level identity; derive phase from calendar time, not from whether a reminder fired.**

Fact: RFC 5545 distinguishes date-only from date-time events, defines a non-inclusive DTEND, and specifies recurrence, exceptions and time-zone semantics. [RFC 5545](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545)

Recommendation: upcoming → in progress → ended, with cancelled/deleted and unknown/stale branches. Define “starting soon” as an editable presentation window, not a stored truth. Preserve series ID plus occurrence identity/recurrence reference; store instants and original zone/date semantics. All-day entries remain date ranges, not midnight emotional prompts. Recompute on edits, recurrence exceptions, wake, clock/zone change and permission loss. Never infer attendance or completion from end time.

Limits: Test DST gaps/overlaps, floating times, overnight and zero-duration entries, overlapping meetings, moved recurring instances and events spanning travel. Ended is not “completed”; stale or unavailable calendars must remain visibly stale.

6. **Separate calendar authorization, reminder authorization and emotional check-in consent; make notification delivery best-effort and entirely user controlled.**

Fact: Apple’s WWDC23 privacy session distinguishes creation-only/write-only access from reading existing calendars, and its notification documentation requires authorization and checking settings. [WWDC23 privacy](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10053/); [Notification permission](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications)

Recommendation: ask calendar access only after “Connect calendar”; reading requires full access, with a clear explanation and manual-only denial fallback. Ask notification permission after a preview. Default emotional prompts off; when enabled, offer per-calendar/event-type controls, quiet hours, lead time, snooze, skip and one-click pause. Use stable request IDs, cancel stale requests, suppress duplicates and expire prompts rather than replaying a backlog after wake.

Limits: App-level calendar selection is not an OS-enforced narrow read permission. Focus, disabled notifications, sleep and system scheduling can suppress/delay delivery; never bypass Focus or claim reliable alarms. Notification bodies should hide sensitive titles by default.

7. **Use a very short optional emotional self-report; do not present a custom slider as a validated instrument.**

Fact: a Dutch 14-day research cohort had 94.3% median adherence, whereas a 28-day student protocol averaged 67% completion and 16% dropout. Neither predicts consumer use. [Dutch EMA cohort](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm9020322); [28-day protocol](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0001348)

Recommendation: one five-point pleasant–unpleasant question, optional separate activation question, and distinct skip/not sure/prefer-not-to-answer controls. Anchor timing: “How did this event feel?” versus “How do you feel now?” Store which question was answered and response latency. Default cap: one invitation/day, user-adjustable, no streaks, no escalating reminders; details and notes only after deliberate expansion.

Limits: The proposed short form is a design hypothesis, not an equivalent of EMOWI or any diagnostic scale. Pilot comprehension, accessibility and burden; do not promise a particular completion rate or claim calm versus energized is a pure measure of physiological arousal.

8. **Represent missingness honestly and keep calendar-event check-ins separate from general mood sampling.**

Fact: respondents missed EMA prompts because they were busy or asleep; branching questionnaires may incentivize reporting no affect. A within-person outpatient study found different valence/arousal distributions under event-triggered versus random sampling. [Burden and omissions](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm9020322); [Affective yield](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2022.05.015); [Sampling comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7928529/)

Recommendation: retain eligible/offered/answered/skipped/expired states, prompt type and timestamps locally. Show “6 answers from 14 offered; 23 eligible events,” rather than “your mood this week.” Never impute neutral, carry forward a mood, treat no response as recovery or pool random prompts with event check-ins. Keep retrospective edits distinct from contemporaneous reports.

Limits: Missingness can be informative, but the app cannot know why any particular answer is absent. Calendar selection, event salience, voluntary use and notification suppression all bias the observed sample; no causal or diagnostic interpretation.

9. **Offer deliberate reframing as one optional perspective—not an instruction to feel better. Preserve the original appraisal and an equally accessible “leave it as it is” path.**

Fact: short laboratory experiments support some immediate effects of reappraisal, but acceptance was less difficult in another comparison. A controllability study found opposite depression associations depending on stressor context; it was not a causal trial. [Reappraisal experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21999941/); [Acceptance comparison](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6188704/); [Context dependence](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797613496434/)

Recommendation: after user initiation, ask “Is there something practical you want to change?” Offer plan/action, acknowledge/accept, another plausible perspective, or stop. Use prompts such as “What else might be true, without denying what happened?” and “What support or boundary would help?” Save alternate wording beside—not over—the original. No automatic positive rewrite or AI generation in v2.

Limits: This is a writing aid, not treatment or medical advice. Do not reframe away danger, mistreatment or a real obligation; do not score an emotional improvement. Laboratory effects do not establish lasting app benefits, and forced positivity can feel invalidating.

10. **Use tagged GitHub prereleases as the canonical binary channel, a maintained personal Homebrew tap for convenience, and documented source builds for inspectability.**

Fact: Maccy documents GitHub/Homebrew installation; Homebrew specifies cask metadata and acceptance rules. GitHub immutable releases lock assets and tags and generate release attestations. [Maccy](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/README.md); [Cask Cookbook](https://docs.brew.sh/Cask-Cookbook); [Homebrew acceptance](https://docs.brew.sh/Adding-Software-to-Homebrew); [Immutable releases](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/concepts/supply-chain-security/immutable-releases)

Recommendation: release 0.2.0-beta.N with source commit, release notes, minimum macOS, architectures, known issues, migration/rollback instructions, checksums and dashboard artifact. Draft and attach every asset before publishing an immutable release. Create an own-tap cask with version, exact versioned URL, sha256, name, desc, homepage, app artifact and macOS dependency; test install/upgrade/uninstall on clean machines. Provide pinned toolchain/dependencies and explicit development-signing instructions for source builds.

Limits: An experimental project is not entitled to inclusion in homebrew/cask. Never use no_check or disable Gatekeeper as a convenience fix. Checksums/attestations establish artifact identity and provenance, not absence of vulnerabilities or reproducible builds.

11. **Sign and notarize the public macOS binary; keep developer builds clearly distinct and protect release credentials.**

Fact: Apple notarization scans Developer ID-signed software and issues a ticket; it is not App Review. The documented workflow uses Hardened Runtime, appropriate entitlements and current notarytool/stapler tooling. [Apple notarization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/security/notarizing_macos_software_before_distribution)

Recommendation: build/test → sign nested code and final app with Developer ID Application and timestamp → package a DMG → submit with xcrun notarytool submit --wait → inspect status/log → staple and validate the ticket → test Gatekeeper on a clean, quarantined download → hash the final artifact → upload/publish. Restrict signing secrets to protected release jobs, minimize workflow permissions and use manual approval. Local development may use a separately documented development signature without distributing the private certificate.

Limits: Notarization is not a security audit or efficacy approval. Do not promise reproducibility until independently rebuilt bytes match. A clean source build still requires correct local permissions/signing; never tell users to globally disable Gatekeeper.

12. **Ship declarative custom configuration in v2; defer arbitrary executable plugins and public plugin auto-update.**

Fact: Hammerspoon offers powerful Lua configuration and Spoons, including installation from repositories and arbitrary URLs. Its documentation does not establish a sandbox or review guarantee. [Getting started](https://www.hammerspoon.org/go/); [SpoonInstall](https://www.hammerspoon.org/Spoons/SpoonInstall.html)

Recommendation: versioned JSON/TOML configuration for categories, weights, phase windows, quiet hours and retention, with schema validation, preview, reset and safe-mode launch. Preserve a working last-known config. Publish examples with no secrets. Later extensions should have explicit capabilities, narrow input schemas, version compatibility and user-reviewed local installation; never execute imported dashboard data as code.

Limits: A plugin loaded inside the app normally inherits its access. Do not advertise isolation without implementing and testing it. Hammerspoon is a useful power-user prototype path, not a reason to require Accessibility for a read-only calendar dashboard.

## Source ledger

### S1 · [ActivityWatch Privacy Policy](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/privacy.html)

Date: Undated; accessed 2026-10-16

Evidence: Documents local storage, no third-party behavioral analytics, disableable release checks, and an optional explicitly enabled third-party AI feature.

Authority: Official project documentation; primary source for stated behavior.

Independence: Maintainer-authored; not independent verification.

Limits: Policy is not a code or network audit; current optional features may differ from older releases.

### S2 · [ActivityWatch — Security](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/security.html)

Date: undated

Evidence: ActivityWatch states it handles highly sensitive data and has not undergone security audits. By default, its API lacks authentication; reachable processes can read, write, and delete data. Optional API-key authentication is limited to aw-server-rust, disabled by default on desktop, and does not protect database or configuration files. The API uses plain HTTP. Optional SQLCipher requires a specially built aw-server-rust and is absent from release builds.

Authority: First-party ActivityWatch security documentation; source-country provenance is not stated.

Independence: Not independent; the project documents its own security properties and limitations.

Limits: Undated and not an audit; details may vary by implementation or version. It does not establish protection from local malware, other users, copied databases, or exposed network bindings.

### S3 · [ActivityWatch — Architecture](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/architecture.html)

Date: undated

Evidence: aw-server stores and retrieves activities in buckets and hosts the Web UI through a REST API. The server does not collect data itself; watcher processes observe data and send it through aw-client. The project provides Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, and Rust clients, a web UI, and an experimental Tauri alternative embedding aw-server-rust.

Authority: First-party ActivityWatch architecture documentation; source-country provenance is not stated.

Independence: Not independent; it is a project-authored descriptive architecture account.

Limits: Undated and not a security guarantee. It does not quantify reliability, resolve duplicate or conflicting watcher events, or establish cross-version compatibility.

### S4 · [Hammerspoon — Getting Started](https://www.hammerspoon.org/go/)

Date: undated

Evidence: Hammerspoon bridges macOS system APIs into Lua and requires Accessibility permission. Spoons are Lua plugins distributed through the Hammerspoon Spoons site or repository. The documented flow downloads a zip, opens the Spoon, imports it into ~/.hammerspoon/Spoons/, and loads it from user configuration with hs.loadSpoon().

Authority: First-party Hammerspoon documentation; source-country provenance is not stated.

Independence: Not independent; it describes the project’s own installation and configuration model.

Limits: It does not establish that Spoons are reviewed, signed, sandboxed, or safe. Accessibility permissions and arbitrary plugin or configuration code create local-automation and supply-chain risks.

### S5 · [Hammerspoon — SpoonInstall](https://www.hammerspoon.org/Spoons/SpoonInstall.html)

Date: undated

Evidence: SpoonInstall uses https://github.com/Hammerspoon/Spoons by default and supports registered repositories, synchronous or asynchronous metadata updates, arbitrary zip URLs, and declarative Spoon loading, configuration, starting, and hotkeys. Repository metadata is not persisted and must be updated after restart; synchronous operations block Hammerspoon.

Authority: First-party Hammerspoon API documentation; source-country provenance is not stated.

Independence: Not independent; it documents API mechanics rather than trust or security properties.

Limits: It provides no evidence of signing, review, reproducibility, or repository integrity. Compromise, URL redirection, blocking updates, and post-restart metadata inconsistency remain possible risks.

### S6 · [Maccy — README and installation](https://github.com/p0deje/Maccy/blob/master/README.md)

Date: Undated living README; accessed 2026-10-16

Evidence: The public repository describes a lightweight native macOS clipboard manager, provides downloads through GitHub Releases and Homebrew (brew install maccy), and documents Accessibility permission for automatic pasting. It is a concrete open-source distribution example, not evidence of emotional or productivity efficacy.

Authority: Official maintainer-controlled source repository and installation instructions.

Independence: Project-authored; privacy and performance descriptions are self-reported, not an external audit.

Limits: A successful distribution pattern does not establish this product’s demand, security, or benefit. Accessibility is needed for Maccy’s paste feature, not automatically for a calendar dashboard.

### S7 · [WWDC23: What’s new in privacy](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10053/)

Date: 2023 (WWDC23)

Evidence: Apple describes no-access, write-only, and full-access calendar permissions in macOS Sonoma and iOS 17. EventKitUI can support event creation without calendar permission; write-only access permits creation but not reading existing events, while full access is required to fetch, edit, or delete them. Apple advises requesting full access only for a core feature and handling denial.

Authority: Official Apple developer WWDC presentation; Apple source provenance is United States. Published 2023-06-07.

Independence: Independent primary source for Apple’s EventKit permission model and platform guidance.

Limits: The presentation covers macOS Sonoma and iOS 17, while related samples may be iOS-framed. Behavior should be runtime-gated and tested on target macOS releases; write-only access may prevent post-submission verification.

### S8 · [Asking permission to use notifications](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications)

Date: undated

Evidence: Apple documents requesting authorization before scheduling notifications and treating notification access as user-controlled. UNUserNotificationCenter provides authorization-status and notification-management APIs; denied or otherwise unavailable authorization can prevent notification requests from being submitted or presented.

Authority: Official Apple developer documentation; Apple source provenance is United States.

Independence: Independent primary source for notification-permission requests and user-controlled authorization.

Limits: The page does not guarantee delivery, presentation, or overriding Focus and other system controls. Applications should distinguish requested or scheduled notifications from delivered or user-seen notifications.

### S9 · [RFC 5545: Internet Calendaring and Scheduling Core Object Specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5545)

Date: 2009-09-24

Evidence: RFC 5545 defines DTSTART, DTEND, RRULE, RDATE, EXDATE, RECURRENCE-ID, alarms, and VTIMEZONE. It requires FREQ in RRULEs, prohibits combining COUNT and UNTIL, specifies inclusive UNTIL behavior, and defines timezone, floating-time, DST, recurrence-exception, and invalid or nonexistent-time semantics.

Authority: IETF Standards Track specification; international standards provenance, published 2009-09-24.

Independence: Independent non-Apple primary standards source for iCalendar interchange and recurrence semantics.

Limits: It specifies interchange semantics, not guaranteed behavior across Apple Calendar, EventKit, servers, or other clients. Implementations require interoperability testing and explicit loss or conflict reporting.

### S10 · [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

Date: undated

Evidence: Apple’s privacy guidance supports requesting access in context and explaining why permission is needed. For calendar workflows, this supports just-in-time, purpose-specific prompts and limiting full calendar access to features that genuinely read or modify existing calendar data.

Authority: Official Apple Human Interface Guidelines; Apple source provenance is United States.

Independence: Independent primary Apple UX and privacy guidance source for permission-request design.

Limits: The guidance is UX-oriented and does not establish EventKit behavior, notification delivery, or cross-platform permission semantics. Related WWDC material may provide more specific EventKit implementation detail.

### S11 · [Experienced Burden of and Adherence to Smartphone-Based Ecological Momentary Assessment in Persons with Affective Disorders](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm9020322)

Date: 2020-01-23

Evidence: In a Netherlands Study of Depression and Anxiety sub-study, 384 participants—100 with current affective disorder, 190 remitted, and 94 healthy controls—were invited to complete up to five smartphone EMA questionnaires daily for 14 days. Median adherence was 66 of 70 questionnaires (94.3%) in all groups. Among 321 addendum respondents, missed-questionnaire reasons included being busy (57.3%), no network (22.4%), and asleep (21.2%); server-attributed technical omissions were 0.14%, 0.19%, and 0.30%, respectively. Burden was higher with current disorder (median 1.98/7) than remitted disorder (1.64) or controls (1.28).

Authority: Primary prospective observational research in the Journal of Clinical Medicine, using the Netherlands Study of Depression and Anxiety cohort. Ethical approval, consent, and named institutional affiliations are reported. Funding came from Amsterdam University Medical Centers, the European Commission, Rijksuniversiteit Groningen, Universiteit Leiden, and ZonMw.

Independence: Primary cohort study directly measuring adherence, burden, missed-prompt reasons, and technical omissions. Research assistants, a €20 voucher, and a personalized report supported participation, so adherence reflects a supported protocol.

Limits: Selected Dutch clinical-cohort participants were familiar with smartphones and likely motivated. Monitoring lasted two weeks; prompts were scheduled, responses allowed 60 minutes, and assistants called participants. Adherence measured completion, not truthfulness or accuracy, and may not generalize to longer use, optional prompts, unselected users, or a local-first dashboard without network dependence.

### S12 · [Ecological momentary assessment of affect in depression-prone and control samples: Survey compliance and affective yield](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2022.05.015)

Date: 2022-08-15

Evidence: Primary EMA study of 318 young adults in Hungary—current depression n=28, remitted depression n=168, controls n=122—using five pseudo-random prompts daily for seven days. Overall compliance was 91%; the first daily prompt was lower (86.9%) than prompts 2–5 (90.9–91.66%). Affective yield declined across days: negative affect appeared at 85.8% of prompts and positive affect at 97.9% overall, with lower likelihood by day 7. Additional questions after endorsed affect may have encouraged shorter responses or affect denial.

Authority: Primary research by Andrew J. Seidman, Charles J. George, and Maria Kovacs, affiliated with the University of Pittsburgh and University of Pittsburgh Medical Center, United States; participants were studied in Hungary under University of Pittsburgh and University of Szeged IRB approval. Funded by the U.S. National Institute of Mental Health.

Independence: Independent primary EMA analysis using generalized linear mixed models; the study was not preregistered, and data and syntax were available upon request.

Limits: The sample was 97% Caucasian young adults aged 18–31, with an unbalanced depressed group and prior research involvement. Prompts were scheduled, not event-triggered; staff contact occurred during the protocol. Social context followed affect endorsement, preventing predictor analysis. The proposed explanation for declining affect reports is speculative, and findings do not establish clinical efficacy.

### S13 · [Validation of a transdiagnostic psychopathology ecological momentary assessment protocol in a university student sample](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0001348)

Date: 2025

Evidence: Primary 28-day study of 262 university students (mean age 21.9; 84.8% female) receiving morning, eight momentary, evening, and weekly surveys; momentary surveys could contain 35 items. Average completion was 67% (162 of 236 surveys); surveys took 1 minute 46 seconds, and 16% dropped out, with 66% of dropouts in week one. Completion declined later and on weekends. Higher baseline psychopathology predicted burden, not compliance or dropout. The study supports measurement and network-modeling aims, not clinical efficacy.

Authority: Primary Psychological Assessment article, Vol. 37, Nos. 1–2, pp. 46–61. Authors were affiliated with Maastricht University and Leiden University, Netherlands. The study was ethically approved, preregistered, and shared code and materials through OSF. Funding came from the Netherlands Organisation for Scientific Research and Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture and Science. Authors stated no known conflicts.

Independence: Independent primary longitudinal study evaluating the authors’ transdiagnostic EMA protocol in university students; it reports new feasibility, compliance, subjective-experience, variability, and validity analyses.

Limits: Predominantly female university students from three Dutch universities limit representativeness. The intensive fixed protocol, expiring surveys, and incentive-linked rewards may not generalize to optional, lower-frequency local-first dashboards. Sixteen percent dropped out; average compliance was 67%, with no universal acceptable EMA threshold. Associations are correlational and do not establish diagnosis, treatment benefit, or clinical efficacy.

### S14 · [Creation and validation of the pictorial ecological momentary well-being instrument (EMOWI) for adolescents](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8739680/)

Date: 2022-01-07

Evidence: Mixed-methods development study of an adolescent EMA well-being instrument. Qualitative work with French and Canadian professionals and adolescents supported face validity and favored the pictorial version. Among French 8th graders, the final eight-item scale supported one factor (RMSEA 0.072; CFI 0.937; TLI 0.912), internal consistency (alpha 0.85), intraday reliability (ICC 0.83), and expected correlations (WEMWBS 0.67; ADRS −0.55; HADS −0.47). Item ceiling effects occurred, but not for the global score; boredom was removed for weak loading.

Authority: Primary peer-reviewed Quality of Life Research article (2022;31:1849–1858), with authors affiliated with Université de Lorraine, CHRU-Nancy/INSERM/Université de Lorraine, and Montréal public-health institutions. Quantitative validation involved 387 pictorial- and 383 verbal-version French school participants. Funded by the French National Cancer Institute and Public Health Research Institute.

Independence: Independent instrument-development study using literature review, expert review, cognitive interviews, and psychometric analyses. Yan Kestens held shares in Polygon Research Inc., which marketed the administration application; other authors reported no relevant interests.

Limits: The authors explicitly state that the instrument was not evaluated in a true EMA deployment. Quantitative testing occurred only in France, although Canadian adolescents contributed qualitative development. Participants were mainly 13- and 14-year-old 8th graders. Ceiling effects occurred on individual items, and the study does not establish clinical efficacy or improved dashboard use or well-being.

### S15 · [Comparing Signal-Contingent and Event-Contingent Experience Sampling Ratings of Affect in a Sample of Psychotherapy Outpatients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7928529/)

Date: 2019-11-27

Evidence: In a within-person study, 40 psychotherapy outpatients completed 21 days of concurrent sampling: six random prompts during waking hours and event-contingent reports after face-to-face social interactions over three minutes. Across 8,877 reports, event-contingent ratings had higher pleasant valence (72.73 vs. 64.04) and arousal (63.98 vs. 54.01). Signal-contingent ratings had higher arousal variability, while valence variability did not differ significantly; signal-contingent entropy was also higher (5.73 vs. 5.53). The schedules produced different affective landscapes and should not be treated interchangeably.

Authority: Primary research by Sindes Dawood and colleagues, all identified with Pennsylvania State University, University Park, Pennsylvania, USA. The article reports institutional funding and explicitly states no conflict of interest.

Independence: Independent primary within-sample, within-person comparison in which participants used both schedules concurrently for 21 days.

Limits: Small convenience sample of community mental-health outpatients: 36 women, 4 men, and 90% Caucasian, with frequent anxiety, mood, borderline personality, and other diagnoses. Fourteen of 54 recruits were excluded; missingness was 27.68% for signal and 29.85% for possible event reports. Events were only face-to-face interactions, and findings may not generalize to other populations, schedules, rates, measures, or intervals.

### S16 · [Cognitive reappraisal and acceptance: an experimental comparison of two emotion regulation strategies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21999941/)

Date: 2011-10-06

Evidence: In an experiment with 94 participants randomized to reappraisal, acceptance, or watch-control groups, participants viewed aversive film clips. Compared with watching only, both strategies significantly reduced subjective distress, aversive physiological reactions, and behavioral avoidance. Differences between reappraisal and acceptance were few and favored reappraisal when present. In reappraisal, elicited aversive emotion correlated positively with avoidance; no such correlation occurred with acceptance. These short-term laboratory findings do not establish clinical efficacy or dashboard effectiveness.

Authority: PubMed-indexed original research in Behaviour Research and Therapy, 49(12), 858–866, DOI 10.1016/j.brat.2011.09.011. Authors were Martin Wolgast, Lars-Gunnar Lundh, and Gardar Viborg. The supplied text does not provide affiliations, countries, or conflicts.

Independence: Independent original randomized laboratory experiment; the supplied record identifies non-U.S. government research support and no dependence on other listed studies.

Limits: Used instructed strategies, aversive films, and immediate outcomes; clinical composition is unspecified in the abstract. Brief artificial induction and absent longer-term or clinical outcomes limit translation to everyday dashboards. The supplied text omits group sizes, detailed randomization, effect sizes, affiliations, and conflicts.

### S17 · [Cognitive Reappraisal and Acceptance: Effects on Emotion, Physiology, and Perceived Cognitive Costs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6188704/)

Date: 2017-11-20

Evidence: In two within-subjects laboratory samples (N=142; first-year students and community participants), people watched sad films while using acceptance or reappraisal. Reappraisal produced larger decreases in negative and increases in positive emotion during clips and recovery. Acceptance was perceived as less difficult and, in one sample, produced less skin-conductance dampening during clips. Sample 1 found greater reappraisal effects during films but no post-recovery self-report difference; acceptance was less difficult and more successful, with greater skin conductance during films. Procedures used approximately two-minute films and recoveries.

Authority: Peer-reviewed Emotion article, 18(1), 58–74, DOI 10.1037/emo0000371. Affiliations included Franklin & Marshall College, Teachers College Columbia University, NYU School of Medicine, LaSalle University, and University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. Franklin & Marshall’s IRB approved the study.

Independence: Original laboratory study using two samples and randomized within-person strategy order; it was not a clinical trial or dashboard evaluation.

Limits: Findings concern brief instructed responses to sad films, not durable everyday behavior or clinical outcomes. ## Counter-evidence

- Local-first can still leak sensitive data: ActivityWatch explicitly documents unauthenticated reachable APIs, plaintext transport and no security audit. Do not reproduce its defaults or claim local storage means encrypted storage. [Security](https://docs.activitywatch.net/en/latest/security.html)
- EMA adherence is protocol-dependent: 94.3% median adherence in a short supported Dutch cohort contrasts with 67% mean completion and 16% dropout in a longer student protocol. These are not like-for-like trials and cannot be used to forecast this app’s retention. [Dutch cohort](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm9020322) [Student protocol](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0001348)
- Short surveys are not automatically truthful: an EMA study discusses the possibility that reporting no affect avoids follow-up questions. Missing and zero-valued responses require distinct handling. [Affective yield](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jad.2022.05.015)
- Event-linked reports are selected observations, not an unbiased emotional day: event- and signal-contingent sampling yielded different affect distributions in the same 40 outpatients. The sample and interaction-trigger definition limit transfer to calendar events. [Sampling study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7928529/)
- Reappraisal is not universally superior: acceptance can be easier; controllability moderated associations with depression; authenticity results varied by strategy and measure. These findings argue for user choice, not against every use of reframing.
